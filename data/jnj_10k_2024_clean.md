## PART I

### Item 1. Business

#### General

Johnson & Johnson and its subsidiaries (the Company) have approximately 138,100 employees worldwide engaged in the research and development, manufacture and sale of a broad range of products in the healthcare field. Johnson & Johnson is a holding company, incorporated in the State of New Jersey in 1887, with operating companies conducting business in virtually all countries of the world. The Company's primary focus is products related to human health and well-being.

The Chief Operating Decision Maker (CODM) is the Company's Chief Executive Officer. The Executive Committee is Johnson & Johnson's senior leadership team responsible for setting strategy and driving accountability.

#### Segments of Business

The Company is organized into two business segments: **Innovative Medicine** and **MedTech**.

**Innovative Medicine** focuses on the following therapeutic areas:
- Immunology (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, psoriasis)
- Infectious Diseases (e.g., HIV/AIDS)
- Neuroscience (e.g., mood disorders, neurodegenerative disorders, schizophrenia)
- Oncology (e.g., prostate cancer, hematologic malignancies, lung cancer, bladder cancer)
- Cardiovascular and Metabolism (e.g., thrombosis, diabetes, macular degeneration)
- Pulmonary Hypertension (e.g., Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension)

Key Innovative Medicine products include: REMICADE (infliximab), SIMPONI/SIMPONI ARIA (golimumab), STELARA (ustekinumab), TREMFYA (guselkumab), EDURANT (rilpivirine), PREZISTA/PREZCOBIX (darunavir), SYMTUZA, CONCERTA (methylphenidate), INVEGA SUSTENNA/TRINZA (paliperidone palmitate), SPRAVATO (esketamine), CARVYKTI (ciltacabtagene autoleucel), ZYTIGA (abiraterone acetate), ERLEADA (apalutamide), IMBRUVICA (ibrutinib), DARZALEX/DARZALEX FASPRO (daratumumab), TECVAYLI (teclistamab), XARELTO (rivaroxaban), OPSUMIT (macitentan), UPTRAVI (selexipag).

**MedTech** includes products used in:
- Cardiovascular (electrophysiology, Abiomed heart recovery, Shockwave circulatory restoration, neurovascular)
- Orthopaedics (hips, knees, trauma, spine, sports)
- Surgery (advanced and general surgery, Mentor breast aesthetics)
- Vision (ACUVUE contact lenses, TECNIS intraocular lenses)

#### Geographic Areas

The Company conducts business in virtually all countries worldwide. In fiscal 2024, approximately 43% of sales occurred outside the U.S. (approximately 23% in Europe, 5% in the Western Hemisphere excluding U.S., and 15% in Asia-Pacific and Africa).

#### Raw Materials

Raw materials essential to the Company's business are generally readily available from multiple sources.

#### Patents

The Company's subsidiaries own or are licensed under a significant number of patents in the U.S. and other countries. Key patent facts:
- **DARZALEX** (daratumumab + DARZALEX FASPRO): ~13.1% of total 2024 revenues. Core patents expire in the U.S. in 2029 and extend to 2031/2032 in select European countries.
- **STELARA** (ustekinumab): ~11.7% of total 2024 revenues. Biosimilar launches expected in Europe and the U.S. in 2025.

#### Trademarks

The Company sells products under registered trademarks protected in the U.S. and internationally.

#### Seasonality

Worldwide sales do not reflect significant seasonality; however, spending is typically heavier in Q4, principally for advertising and R&D activity.

#### Competition

The Company's subsidiaries compete with companies both locally and globally across all product lines. Competition in research — internal and external — is particularly significant. The competitive environment requires substantial ongoing investment in R&D.

#### Environment

The Company believes its operations comply in all material respects with applicable environmental laws and regulations, and that compliance is not expected to have a material effect on capital expenditures, cash flows, or earnings.

#### Regulation

The Company's businesses are subject to varying degrees of governmental regulation worldwide. Key regulatory matters:
- U.S. FDA regulation of pharmaceutical products and medical technology (product safety, efficacy, manufacturing, advertising, labeling, safety reporting)
- Inflation Reduction Act (IRA, 2022): CMS authorized to negotiate Medicare prices for high-spend drugs starting in 2026 (Part D) and 2028 (Part B). XARELTO, STELARA, and IMBRUVICA are on the first Selected Drug list.
- Janssen filed litigation in July 2023 challenging the IRA's constitutionality under the First and Fifth Amendments; appeal filed to the Third Circuit in April 2024.
- EU regulations: NIS2, EHDS, Data Act, Cyber Resilience Act, AI Act — increasing privacy and cybersecurity compliance requirements.
- China data security and personal information protection regulations.
- 340B Drug Pricing Program requirements.

### Item 1A. Risk Factors

#### Risks Related to Business, Industry, and Operations

**Competitive pressures:** The Company faces competition on cost-effectiveness, technological innovation, IP rights, product performance, pricing, and reimbursement. Loss of patent exclusivity for Innovative Medicine products often leads to substantial revenue declines from generic/biosimilar entrants.

**Manufacturing interruptions:** 64 manufacturing facilities and thousands of suppliers globally. Disruptions can stem from regulatory action, labor disputes, natural disasters, raw material shortages, or pandemics.

**Third-party manufacturing reliance:** The Company depends on third-party manufacturers for certain raw materials, components, and products. Failure by these parties could result in delays and increased costs.

**Counterfeit products:** Counterfeit versions of Company products pose patient safety risks and may damage reputation and revenues.

**Global health crises:** Pandemics and epidemics could adversely impact sales, supply chain, clinical trial operations, and financial condition.

#### Risks Related to Government Regulation and Legal Proceedings

**Healthcare pricing reforms:** Downward pricing pressure from IRA, Medicaid, Medicare, and international government price controls. CMS-established pricing for selected drugs begins in 2026.

**Regulatory compliance costs:** Increasing number of complex laws and regulations in the U.S. and internationally. Non-compliance risks include product withdrawals, fines, debarment, and criminal prosecution.

**Legal proceedings:** The Company faces numerous lawsuits including product liability (notably talc/JOHNSON'S Baby Powder), patent disputes, antitrust, and government investigations. Talc litigation reserve: approximately $11.6 billion ($13.5 billion nominal).

**Tax law changes:** OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax (15%) enacted in several countries effective January 1, 2024. Net impact to Company's 2024 effective tax rate was less than 1%.

#### Risks Related to Intellectual Property

**Patent challenges:** Third parties routinely challenge the Company's patents seeking to market generic or biosimilar products. Successful challenges could result in rapid market share and revenue losses.

**Public policy:** Increasingly unfavorable toward IP rights, particularly outside the U.S. (e.g., Russia allowing exploitation of patents without consent).

#### Risks Related to Product Development

**Innovation challenges:** New products introduced in the past five years accounted for approximately 25% of 2024 sales. Development of successful new products is uncertain and costly.

**AI-related risks:** Use of data science, machine learning, and generative AI creates risks related to data privacy, security, obsolescence, and evolving regulation.

#### Risks Related to Financial and Economic Conditions

**Foreign currency exposure:** ~43% of 2024 sales outside the U.S. A 1% change in the U.S. Dollar relative to all foreign currencies would impact translated foreign sales by ~$0.4 billion.

**Highly inflationary economies:** Argentina, Turkey, Venezuela, and Egypt (added Q4 2024) accounted for as highly inflationary (prior 3-year cumulative inflation >100%).

**Geopolitical risks:** Russia-Ukraine war, Middle East conflict, and U.S.-China tensions affect supply chain, market access, and financial performance. U.S. tariffs on products from China, Mexico, and Canada announced.

**Credit ratings:** The Company maintains investment-grade ratings from Moody's and S&P. Any downgrade could increase borrowing costs and limit capital market access.

#### Other Risks

**Talent:** Competitive market for skilled employees. Unsuccessful succession planning could adversely affect operations.

**Climate change:** Physical risks to facilities, supply chain disruption, water scarcity, and potential new regulatory requirements for greenhouse gas reduction.

**Cybersecurity:** Growing volume and sophistication of cyberattacks. CIO and CISO oversee cybersecurity risk. RCSC of Board provides quarterly oversight. No material cybersecurity incidents reported to date.

**Data privacy:** Subject to global privacy and data protection laws. Breach risks include substantial fines, class actions, and reputational damage.

### Item 1C. Cybersecurity

The Company has documented cybersecurity policies and standards, assesses risks from cybersecurity threats, and maintains a cybersecurity incident response plan. Key elements:
- Security tools supporting protection, detection, and response capabilities
- Third-party risk management program
- Mandatory employee training on phishing, email security, and data privacy
- Periodic third-party maturity assessments of the information security program
- CISO (25+ years of experience) reports quarterly to the Regulatory Compliance & Sustainability Committee (RCSC) of the Board
- Board oversees overall risk management, including cybersecurity risks, via the RCSC

### Selected Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements

#### Note 1: Summary of Significant Accounting Policies (Selected)

**Revenue recognition:** Recognized when control of goods transfers to customers. Global payment terms: 30–90 days. Rebates, returns, promotions, and discounts treated as variable consideration and recorded as reductions to sales. Sales returns reserve: ~1.0% of annual net trade sales.

**Managed Care/Medicare/Medicaid rebate liability:** $12.3 billion (2024) and $11.5 billion (2023).

**Inventories:** Stated at lower of cost or net realizable value; first-in, first-out method.

**Goodwill and intangibles:** Indefinite-lived assets tested for impairment annually (Q4); IPR&D from business combinations treated as indefinite-lived until project completes.

**R&D:** Expensed as incurred. Upfront/milestone payments pre-approval expensed; post-approval capitalized and amortized.

**Depreciation:**
- Buildings and building equipment: 30 years
- Land and leasehold improvements: 10–20 years
- Machinery and equipment: 2–13 years
- Capitalized software: 3–8 years

**Dividends:** Increased for 62nd consecutive year; $4.91/share paid in 2024.

**TCJA (2017 Tax Reform):** Final payment of ~$2.5B due in fiscal 2025. Company elected deferred method for GILTI accounting.

**Supplier Finance Programs:** $788M in confirmed obligations under supplier finance programs as of December 29, 2024 (vs. $704M at year-end 2023).

**Annual Closing Date:** Fiscal year ends on the Sunday nearest end of December; normally 52 weeks (53 weeks every 5–6 years; next 53-week year: fiscal 2026).
